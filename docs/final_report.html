Section 1: Revised Kick-Off Document

1.1 Project Overview & Background

Students and corporate employers coordinate internship recruitment and operational placements each semester. However, the existing process is predominantly managed through disconnected emails, spreadsheets, messaging applications (e.g., LINE, Discord), and paper forms. This ad-hoc approach creates substantial operational friction: applications are misplaced, candidate statuses remain ambiguous, working hours go unmonitored during the term, and performance assessments are often compiled hurriedly at the conclusion of the placement.

InternFlow is a modern, cloud-native, centralized internship and Cooperative and Work-Integrated Education (CWIE) management platform. It unifies the entire lifecycle of student internships into an accountable, transparent, and auditable digital environment.

Core Value Propositions

  • Centralized Corporate Job Board: Verified employer postings featuring clear job descriptions, required competencies, department classifications, compensation/allowance figures, working hours, and work modality (On-site, Remote, Hybrid).
  • Multi-Stage Application & Interview Pipeline: Real-time transparent tracking of candidate applications from initial submission, HR screening, interview scheduling (INTERVIEW_SCHEDULED), offer extension, single-placement exclusivity commitment, through company-approved cancellation.
  • Single Placement & Exclusivity Rule: While candidates can receive multiple offers, they are limited to committing to exactly ONE official placement (COMMITTED), which automatically declines competing offers, eliminating candidate hoarding and placement abandonment.
  • Multi-Artifact Document Attachments: Scalable file upload pipeline for CVs, portfolios, academic transcripts, and external project links (GitHub, Figma, Google Drive).
  • CWIE Operational Daily & Weekly Logbook: Daily activity journaling with attendance modality tracking, pin-point task lists, problem-solving reflections, and automatic backdated submission detection.
  • Company Mentor Review & Sign-Off: Supervisor inspection drawer enabling day-by-day and batch weekly approvals, 1–5 star performance ratings, and qualitative mentorship feedback.
  • Standardized 3-Dimensional Performance Rubric: Final evaluation evaluating Work Quality, Punctuality & Responsibility, and Communication & Teamwork, yielding an official printable Internship Completion Certificate & Performance Report.
  • Seamless Multilingual Support: Dynamic instant language switching between Thai (TH) and English (EN) across all system interfaces, forms, and status notifications.

1.2 Problem Statement & Current Shortcomings

The conventional internship recruitment and monitoring process suffers from several structural inefficiencies:

  1. Absence of a Single Source of Truth: Students must scour disparate social media groups, university bulletin boards, and corporate career sites. There is no unified portal listing accredited positions with explicit allowance and modality terms.
  2. Untraceable Application Lifecycle: Once an application is submitted via email or Google Forms, students have no visibility into its status. HR recruiters are burdened by repetitive inquiry emails, while students remain uncertain whether to accept competing opportunities.
  3. Absence of a Structured Interview Stage: Informal communication regarding interview arrangements often leads to missed appointments, forgotten meeting links, and mismatched expectations between hiring managers and applicants.
  4. Offer Hoarding and Unannounced Dropouts: Students frequently hold multiple offers simultaneously until the start date, subsequently dropping out without notice and leaving employers with unfilled positions.
  5. Unmonitored Daily/Weekly Progress: Students typically compile paper logbooks or spreadsheets retroactively at the end of the internship period. Mentors are unable to verify attendance, daily deliverables, or technical roadblocks in real time.
  6. Superficial Performance Appraisals: End-of-term evaluations are often completed as an administrative checkbox rather than a constructive, rubric-governed assessment.
  7. Fragmented Document Management: Exchanging PDF resumes, portfolios, and signed agreement letters via email threads introduces security vulnerabilities, version confusion, and storage headaches.

1.4 Functional Requirements (Comprehensive & Updated)

Subsystem 1: User Management & Authentication System

  • FR-1.1: Secure registration and login using email and password credentials with server-side validation.
  • FR-1.2: Passwords must be hashed using bcrypt (minimum 10 salt rounds) prior to storage; plaintext passwords must never be persisted.
  • FR-1.3: Issue stateless JSON Web Tokens (JWT) signed with HMAC-SHA256 and a 24-hour expiration upon successful authentication.
  • FR-1.4: Enforce Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) with predefined roles: STUDENT and COMPANY_HR.
  • FR-1.5: Automatically initialize role-specific profile records (StudentProfile or CompanyProfile) upon user creation.
  • FR-1.6: Mask sensitive academic fields (e.g., student GPA) from unauthorized public views, exposing them only to authenticated HR recruiters.
  • FR-1.7: Enable public profile inspection of platform participants through slide-over modals (PublicProfileModal).

Subsystem 2: Job Listing & Discovery Management

  • FR-2.1: Enable Company HR to create, edit, toggle active status, and delete internship postings.
  • FR-2.2: Each posting must capture: Title, Department, Description, Requirements, Location, Work Modality (isRemote), Openings Count, Allowance/Compensation, Working Hours/Schedule, and Contact Information (Email, Phone, LINE ID).
  • FR-2.3: Provide transactional cascade deletion of job postings and associated application records with explicit in-app confirmation modals.
  • FR-2.4: Enable students to browse active job openings with full-text keyword search and multi-criteria filters (Department, Work Modality, Allowance).

Subsystem 3: Multi-Stage Application, Interview & Placement Pipeline

  • FR-3.1: Enable students to apply for jobs with cover letters, direct file uploads (CVs, transcripts), and external URLs.
  • FR-3.2: Strictly enforce the One Active Application Per Company Rule through database-level compound unique constraints (studentId + jobPostId).
  • FR-3.3 (Interview Stage): Enable Company HR to transition candidates from PENDING or REVIEWING to INTERVIEW_SCHEDULED.
  • FR-3.4 (Interview Notice): When an application enters INTERVIEW_SCHEDULED, the student's portal must display an Indigo Notice Banner instructing them that HR will coordinate date, time, and meeting details via email or phone.
  • FR-3.5 (Offer Extension): Company HR can extend an official offer (ACCEPTED) either directly from review or following the interview stage.
  • FR-3.6 (Single Placement Commitment): Students can commit to exactly ONE offer (COMMITTED). Committing automatically executes an atomic database transaction that sets all other active applications for that student to REJECTED / closed.
  • FR-3.7 (Controlled Cancellation Workflow): A committed student cannot unilaterally abandon their placement. They must submit a cancellation request stating the reason (CANCEL_REQUESTED). The placement is only released once HR explicitly confirms (CANCELLED).
  • FR-3.8: Complete Standardized Status Lifecycle: $$\text{PENDING} \longrightarrow \text{REVIEWING} \longrightarrow \text{INTERVIEW_SCHEDULED} \longrightarrow \text{ACCEPTED} \longrightarrow \text{COMMITTED} \underset{\text{Reject}}{\stackrel{\text{Approve}}{\rightleftarrows}} \text{CANCEL_REQUESTED} \longrightarrow \text{CANCELLED}$$

Subsystem 4: CWIE Daily & Weekly Operational Logbook

  • FR-4.1: Access to the CWIE Logbook is strictly locked until a student possesses an active, confirmed placement (COMMITTED or CANCEL_REQUESTED).
  • FR-4.2: Enable students to record daily logs specifying Date, Hours Worked (1.0–12.0 hours, default 8.0), Modality (ON_SITE, REMOTE, HYBRID), and Supervisor Name.
  • FR-4.3: Capture itemized task lists (tasksDone), technical troubleshooting notes (problemsAndSolutions), key learnings, and attached artifacts (PDFs, code screenshots, project links).
  • FR-4.4 (Backdated Detection): Automatically detect if a log entry date precedes the submission date; flag the entry as isBackdated = true and display a warning badge in both student and supervisor views.
  • FR-4.5: Enforce uniqueness so a student can only create one log entry per calendar date (@@unique([studentId, logDate])).
  • FR-4.6: Provide real-time calculation of total logged hours versus mentor-approved hours across the internship period.

Subsystem 5: Company Mentor Inspection & Sign-Off

  • FR-5.1: Provide an interactive Inspector Drawer for Company HR/Mentors to examine intern daily logs, completed tasks, and artifact attachments.
  • FR-5.2: Enable mentors to sign off on logs individually or execute batch one-click approvals for an entire week's logs.
  • FR-5.3: Allow mentors to assign 1–5 star ratings and provide qualitative feedback notes that update the student's view in real time.

Subsystem 6: Performance Rubric Evaluation & Certification

  • FR-6.1: Enable Company HR to submit an accredited 3-category performance rubric upon internship completion:
    1. Work Quality & Technical Competence (1–5 Stars)
    2. Punctuality & Professional Responsibility (1–5 Stars)
    3. Communication & Team Collaboration (1–5 Stars)
    4. Qualitative Supervisor Remarks & Feedback
  • FR-6.2: Generate an official, print-ready (window.print()) Internship Completion Certificate & Performance Report consolidating student university metadata, verified hours, supervisor signatures, and rubric scores.

Subsystem 7: Bilingual Internationalization (TH / EN)

  • FR-7.1: Full client-side internationalization with persistent language selection (th / en) via LanguageContext.
  • FR-7.2: Instant UI translation across navigation menus, status badges, forms, modals, error toasts, and printable reports without page reloading.

1.5 Nonfunctional Requirements

1. Performance

  • NFR-1.1: 95% of read API endpoints (GET /api/jobs, GET /api/applications/my) must respond within < 500 ms under normal load.
  • NFR-1.2: Database queries must utilize indexed foreign keys and composite unique constraints to maintain query response times under < 100 ms.
  • NFR-1.3: File uploads (up to 10 MB) must complete streaming to Amazon S3 within < 5 seconds over standard broadband connections.

2. Scalability

  • NFR-2.1: The backend must be stateless to permit horizontal scaling via Amazon ECS Fargate container auto-scaling based on CPU/memory utilization thresholds (70%).
  • NFR-2.2: Database connection pooling managed via Prisma Client to sustain at least 200 concurrent active sessions without connection exhaustion.
  • NFR-2.3: Frontend static bundle served via Amazon CloudFront edge locations with global caching to handle sudden traffic spikes effortlessly.

3. Reliability & Availability

  • NFR-3.1: Target service availability of 99.5% uptime during academic terms.
  • NFR-3.2: Multi-table writes (such as offer acceptance and competing application decline) must execute within atomic database transactions (prisma.$transaction) to prevent orphaned records.
  • NFR-3.3: Amazon RDS Multi-AZ deployment ensures automated failover to a standby replica in a secondary Availability Zone in < 60 seconds in case of infrastructure failure.

4. Data Integrity & Consistency

  • NFR-4.1: Database foreign key constraints enforce referential integrity across users, profiles, jobs, applications, logs, and evaluations.
  • NFR-4.2: Server-side validation rejects invalid status transitions (e.g., transitioning from REJECTED directly to COMMITTED).

5. Security & Privacy

  • NFR-5.1: Passwords must be hashed using bcrypt (10 rounds). Plaintext passwords must never be logged or persisted.
  • NFR-5.2: All protected routes require a valid JWT passed in the HTTP Authorization: Bearer <token> header.
  • NFR-5.3: Sensitive student metadata (GPA) is strictly masked from unauthorized users.
  • NFR-5.4: Amazon S3 buckets are configured with Block Public Access enabled; files are accessed via authenticated backend proxies or pre-signed URLs.
  • NFR-5.5: Cross-Origin Resource Sharing (CORS) is configured to restrict API access exclusively to authorized frontend domains.

6. Usability & Accessibility

  • NFR-6.1: Modern, responsive UI adhering to the Emerald & Mint SaaS design system, fully functional on desktop, tablet, and mobile displays (minimum viewport width 360px).
  • NFR-6.2: Immediate visual feedback provided for all user actions via skeleton loaders, spinners, and interactive toast notifications.

1. Persistent Data Management

  • Database Engine: Amazon RDS PostgreSQL 16 Multi-AZ.
  • Relational Tables: Persists structured business entities across 8 relational tables (User, StudentProfile, CompanyProfile, JobPost, Application, Document, DailyLog, WeeklyLog, InternshipEvaluation).
  • Object / Blob Storage: Binary documents (PDF resumes, transcripts, avatar images, code screenshots) are stored in private Amazon S3 buckets with path prefixes (/resumes/, /transcripts/, /avatars/). The database stores only the immutable S3 URLs (fileUrl).
  • Schema Evolution: Managed through declarative, version-controlled Prisma migrations (prisma migrate dev / prisma migrate deploy), ensuring reproducible database structures across development, staging, and production environments.

2. Transient Data Management

  • Client Session State: Authentication tokens (JWT) and user identity profiles are stored in browser localStorage.
  • Request Scope Data: In-flight HTTP requests carry decoded user identity (req.user = { userId, role }) populated by the authenticate middleware in memory.
  • File Upload Buffers: File uploads are streamed through memory-efficient streaming buffers directly to S3 via multer-s3, avoiding local disk consumption in production container environments.

3. Concurrency Control & Transactional Integrity

  • Atomic Offer Acceptance: When a student accepts an offer, the system must update the chosen application to COMMITTED while updating all other active applications for that student to REJECTED. This is wrapped inside an atomic prisma.$transaction: 
    await prisma.$transaction(async (tx) => {
  // 1. Commit target placement
  await tx.application.update({
    where: { id: targetAppId },
    data: { status: 'COMMITTED' }
  });
  // 2. Automatically decline competing applications
  await tx.application.updateMany({
    where: {
      studentId: student.id,
      id: { not: targetAppId },
      status: { in: ['PENDING', 'REVIEWING', 'INTERVIEW_SCHEDULED', 'ACCEPTED'] }
    },
    data: { status: 'REJECTED' }
  });
});
  • Database Constraints:
    • Candidate spamming prevented by @@unique([studentId, jobPostId]).
    • Duplicate daily logging prevented by @@unique([studentId, logDate]).
    • Foreign key cascade deletion cleans up child records when a parent entity is deleted.

4. Backup & Disaster Recovery

  • Automated Daily Snapshots: Amazon RDS executes automated daily backups with a 35-day retention window.
  • Point-in-Time Recovery (PITR): Transaction logs (WAL) are streamed continuously, enabling restoration to any specific second within the retention window.
  • S3 Bucket Versioning: S3 buckets maintain object versioning to protect against accidental deletion or overwrite.

2.3 Boundary Conditions (Lecture 3 Topics)

Boundary conditions define system behavior during non-steady states: system initialization, termination/shutdown, and unexpected failure modes.

1. System Initialization

  • Cloud Infrastructure Bootstrapping:
    1. Route 53 routes incoming DNS queries to CloudFront (frontend) and ALB (backend).
    2. The Application Load Balancer begins polling the health probe endpoint (GET /api/health).
    3. ECS Fargate pulls container images from Amazon ECR and launches tasks within private subnets.
    4. Node.js backend initializes: loads environment variables (.env), connects to PostgreSQL via Prisma connection pool, verifies schema migrations, and binds to port 4000.
    5. The ALB marks targets as healthy once HTTP 200 is confirmed, opening traffic routing.
  • Client Application Bootstrapping:
    1. The browser requests index.html via CloudFront CDN.
    2. React hydrates and initializes LanguageContext (restoring stored language from localStorage or defaulting to th).
    3. The auth provider inspects localStorage for an existing JWT token. If present and valid, it loads user state; if expired or invalid, it purges stored credentials and directs the user to /login.

2. System Termination

  • Graceful Container Termination (ECS Scale-In / Deployments):
    1. AWS ECS sends a SIGTERM signal to the Node.js process.
    2. The Express server stops accepting new incoming connections on port 4000.
    3. ALB deregisters the target container, routing new traffic to other active tasks.
    4. Active in-flight HTTP requests are given a 30-second draining window to complete.
    5. The Prisma connection pool is gracefully disconnected (await prisma.$disconnect()).
    6. The Node.js process exits cleanly (exit code 0).
  • User Session Termination (Logout):
    1. User clicks "Sign Out" (ออกจากระบบ).
    2. Frontend immediately removes token and user keys from browser localStorage.
    3. Client authentication state is reset to null.
    4. React Router navigates the user to /login with an in-app confirmation toast.

3. Failure Modes & Automated Recovery

  • Primary Database Outage:
    • Symptom: Primary RDS PostgreSQL instance becomes unresponsive.
    • Automated Recovery: Amazon RDS Multi-AZ automatically detects loss of heartbeat and initiates automatic failover to the synchronous standby replica in the secondary Availability Zone. The database DNS endpoint is updated within < 60 seconds without data loss. Prisma connection pools detect dropped connections and reconnect with exponential backoff.
  • Backend Container Crash:
    • Symptom: Uncaught exception or OOM event crashes a Node.js process.
    • Automated Recovery: ECS Fargate task supervisor detects process termination and automatically spawns a replacement container task. The ALB diverts traffic away from the failing task to surviving instances, maintaining zero service downtime.
  • API Error Handling & Stack Trace Masking:
    • Behavior: Centralized Express error-handling middleware catches all unhandled rejections, logs full error details and stack traces to Amazon CloudWatch, and returns sanitized, user-friendly JSON responses ({ error: "Internal server error" }, HTTP 500) to protect internal infrastructure details.
  • Network Disconnection in Client Browser:
    • Behavior: Frontend API client detects network timeouts and surfaces an alert toast ("Unable to connect to server. Please try again.") while preserving form state in component memory, preventing user data loss.

Detailed Design

ER diagram

In this section, PostgreSQL (managed on Amazon RDS for PostgreSQL) is used for our database system which is a relational database management system (RDBMS). The Entity-Relationship (ER) diagram representing the database schema, tables, primary keys (PK), foreign keys (FK), data types, and relational constraints is shown on the following diagram.

  • In the Users table, id is a UUID (String) which defines the primary key (PK) of each user.
  • In the StudentProfiles and CompanyProfiles tables, userId is used as a foreign key (FK) with a unique constraint (1:1 relationship) to define which user account owns each profile.
  • In the JobPosts table, companyProfileId is used as the foreign key (FK) to define which company posted that internship position.
  • In the Applications table, studentId and jobPostId are foreign keys (FK) to define which student submitted the application for which job posting. A composite unique constraint on [studentId, jobPostId] ensures a student cannot submit duplicate applications to the same job posting.
  • In the DailyLogs table, studentId and applicationId are foreign keys (FK) linking daily log entries and attendance hours directly to the student's active placement, with a unique constraint on [studentId, logDate] preventing multiple logs on the same date.
  • In the InternshipEvaluations table, applicationId is used as a foreign key with a 1:1 unique constraint to ensure that each completed internship placement receives exactly one official mentor evaluation.
  • In the Documents table, userId and applicationId are foreign keys linking candidate resumes, transcripts, and portfolios stored in Amazon S3 back to the candidate's application.
  • Note that only salted and hashed passwords (using bcrypt with 10 salt rounds) are stored in the database, not plain text passwords.

3.3 Workflow of the system

The end-to-end operational workflow of InternFlow is illustrated in the diagram below, showing the progression of stages, UI routes, and interactive user actions for both Student applicants and Company HR employers:

Stage 1: Public Discovery & Role Onboarding

  • Guest Landing Page (/): Visitors can explore live verified corporate internship postings, search by keyword, and apply modality (On-site / Remote) and compensation filters without authenticating.
  • Role-Based Registration (/register): New users select their account role (STUDENT or COMPANY_HR), enforce a secure password policy (minimum 6 characters), and submit either academic details or corporate company information.
  • Secure Authentication (/login): Users authenticate with their registered email and bcrypt-hashed password, receiving a signed 24-hour JWT token that routes them to their role-specific dashboard.

Stage 2: Parallel Recruitment & Operational Workflows

Student Workflow (Applicant & Active Intern)

  • Student Step 1: Discover & Search Opportunities (/dashboard/jobs): Students browse and filter positions by department, allowance, and work modality, reviewing company details and clicking "Apply Now".
  • Student Step 2: Submit Application with Resume (Apply Modal): Students upload their CV/Resume PDF (streamed directly to Amazon S3), provide a personal statement, and include external links (GitHub/Figma). The system enforces the One Active Application Per Company Rule to prevent duplicate spamming, initializing the submission as PENDING.
  • Student Step 3: Pipeline & Interview Notice (/dashboard/applications): Students monitor application progress through PENDING ➔ REVIEWING ➔ INTERVIEW_SCHEDULED ➔ ACCEPTED. When scheduled for an interview, an Indigo Interview Notice Card alerts the candidate that HR will reach out with the meeting schedule/link.
  • Student Step 4: Accept Offer & Commit Placement ("Accept Offer & Start"): When an employer extends an offer, the student clicks "Accept Offer". The Atomic Exclusivity Protocol locks the placement to COMMITTED and automatically cancels/declines all other competing applications across the platform.
  • Student Step 5: Daily CWIE Logbook Journaling (/dashboard/logbook): Unlocked exclusively upon holding a COMMITTED placement. Interns record working hours (default 8.0 hrs/day), modality (ON_SITE, REMOTE, HYBRID), completed deliverables, roadblocks, and solutions. Retroactive submissions are automatically flagged with an isBackdated warning badge.

Company HR Workflow (Recruiter & Mentor)

  • HR Step 1: Post & Manage Job Listings (/dashboard/jobs/manage): Recruiters create new listings specifying position title, stipend (THB/month), modality, working hours, and qualifications, immediately publishing them to the live board.
  • HR Step 2: Screen Applicants in Corporate Roster (/dashboard/applicants): HR views candidate profiles, academic GPAs, universities, and downloads verified S3 resumes, transitioning candidates to REVIEWING.
  • HR Step 3: Interview Scheduling & Offer Pipeline (Applicant Card):** HR clicks "Schedule Interview" (INTERVIEW_SCHEDULED) to indicate shortlisting while coordinating the interview date/link externally. Post-interview, HR clicks "Extend Offer" (ACCEPTED) or "Reject" (REJECTED).
  • HR Step 4: Confirmed Placement & Mentorship (Placed Intern): Once the candidate commits, the intern is officially locked into the corporate placement. Unilateral abandonment is blocked; release requires formal student cancellation (CANCEL_REQUESTED) and HR confirmation (CANCELLED).
  • HR Step 5: Interactive Logbook Inspection Drawer (Task Inspector): Mentors click "Inspect Logbook" to open a sliding inspection drawer, reviewing daily logs, hours, and backdated flags, awarding 1–5 star ratings (★) and qualitative feedback individually or in weekly batches.

Stage 3: Performance Rubric Evaluation & Official Certification

  • Company Mentor: Performance Rubric (/dashboard/evaluations): Upon completing the internship duration, supervisors score the intern across three standardized CWIE performance rubric categories (Work Quality, Punctuality & Attendance, and Teamwork — each 1–5 stars) along with final qualitative comments.
  • Official Output: Verified Completion Certificate (Accredited PDF): The system verifies that approved attendance meets the required target threshold (e.g. 300.0–400.0 hours as configured by the company/curriculum) and consolidates logbook hours, mentor ratings, and rubric scores into an official printable Internship Completion Certificate & Final Evaluation Report for academic graduation defense.
